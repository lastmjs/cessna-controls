# Legal notes

This is a plain-language summary of why this project is built the way it is. It is not legal
advice.

## Patents

The Cessna 172 first flew in 1955 and the 175 in 1956. A US utility patent lasts 20 years from
filing and a design patent 14–15 years from grant, so any patents on the original airplane and
its cockpit layout expired decades ago. A patent also only covers making, using, or selling the
invention itself. A picture or a 3D model on a screen that teaches someone what a part is called
does not make or sell an airplane part.

## Copyright

Copyright protects specific creative expression: a photograph, a drawing in the Pilot's
Operating Handbook, a particular 3D model file, a font, a logo. It does not protect facts (what a
mixture knob does, where the throttle is, what color the arcs on an airspeed indicator are) or
functional layouts.

So this project:

- builds every shape from code written for this project, using generic dimensions and common
  knowledge about how these cockpits are arranged;
- draws every gauge face, placard, and display in code, using standard aviation markings;
- copies no photographs, POH illustrations, manuals, textures, or third-party 3D models;
- writes all of its descriptions in its own words.

## Trademarks

"Cessna", "Skyhawk", and "Skylark" are trademarks of Textron Aviation Inc. Using a brand name only
to say what something is ("controls of a Cessna 172") is ordinary descriptive or nominative use.
To stay on the safe side, this project:

- shows no Cessna logos, emblems, or trade dress (the yoke emblem plate is left blank);
- shows no avionics brand names or logos; the radios are generic;
- states clearly that it is not affiliated with or endorsed by Textron Aviation.

## Safety

This is a learning aid for names and locations of controls. It is not a flight training device,
it is not approved by any aviation authority, and it does not replace the Pilot's Operating
Handbook for a specific airplane, a certificated flight instructor, or approved training. Real
airplanes vary by model year and by modification; always check the actual airplane and its
documents.
